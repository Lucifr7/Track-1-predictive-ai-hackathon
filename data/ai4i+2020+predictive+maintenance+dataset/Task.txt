1. TASK 1: DEFINE THE USER & OPERATIONAL CONTEXT
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
- User Persona:
  * Name / Title: Razif, Shift CNC Maintenance Technician
  * Workplace: Precision metal fabrication / CNC machining shop operating 50+
    CNC milling machines cutting Low (L), Medium (M), and High (H) quality parts.
  * Morning Routine & Dilemma:
    Arrives at 7:30 AM with a 45–60 minute pre-shift window before full factory
    production begins. With 50+ machines on the shop floor, he cannot inspect
    every machine each morning.

- Current Problem (Without Our Tool):
  * Blind prioritization: Relies on static weekly schedules or reactive
    firefighting after a machine crashes mid-cycle.
  * Catastrophic mid-shift breakdowns: A worn insert snapping or a motor overheating
    mid-cut halts the line for 2–4 hours and scraps expensive workpieces (especially
    Grade H parts).
  * Unactionable alerts: Standard machine alarms only give generic warning lights or
    raw telemetry numbers without telling Razif which machine is urgent or what
    tools and replacement parts to bring.

- Pitch Persona Statement (for Slides):
  "Our user is Razif, a CNC maintenance technician responsible for dozens of
   milling machines on the factory floor. Each morning, he has less than an hour
   to decide which machines need attention before full production begins.
   Without our tool, he relies on guesswork and fixed schedules—inevitably missing
   brewing tool wear or thermal issues until a machine catastrophically breaks down
   mid-shift."

2. TASK 2: COST MODEL & ASSUMPTIONS (IN MALAYSIAN RINGGIT - RM)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
* IMPORTANT NOTE: All figures below are our explicit engineering assumptions
  (not official industry averages) chosen to model a realistic small-to-medium
  Malaysian machine shop environment. Label them honestly as assumptions in the pitch.

A. Baseline Operational Rates:
- Technician Labor Rate: RM 30 / hour (RM 0.50 / minute, based on ~RM 3,500 - RM 4,500/month salary + overhead)
- CNC Machine Downtime Cost: RM 300 / hour (lost output value, idle operator, schedule delays)
- False Alarm Inspection Cost: RM 15 (30 minutes of technician inspection time finding no defect)

B. Cost Breakdown per Failure Mode (Itemized to Match Totals):
1. Tool Wear Failure (TWF):
   - Planned Fix (Total: RM 100.00):
     15 min labor (RM 7.50) + cutting insert replacement (RM 80.00) + solvent/shims/consumables (RM 12.50).
   - Unexpected Breakdown (Total: RM 650.00):
     Tool snaps mid-cut. Scraps workpiece, clears debris, resets tool and offsets
     (~1.5 hrs downtime @ RM 450.00 + RM 120.00 scrapped part + RM 80.00 replacement tool).

2. Heat Dissipation Failure (HDF):
   - Planned Fix (Total: RM 70.00):
     30 min labor (RM 15.00) + coolant fluid top-up & filter media (RM 55.00).
   - Unexpected Breakdown (Total: RM 850.00):
     Thermal trip shuts down machine mid-cycle; machine cool-down, thermal inspection,
     and recalibration (~2.5 hrs downtime @ RM 750.00 + RM 100.00 fluid flush & labor).

3. Power Failure (PWF):
   - Planned Fix (Total: RM 50.00):
     30 min labor (RM 15.00) + electrical terminal contact cleaner / replacement fuse / belt adjustment (RM 35.00).
   - Unexpected Breakdown (Total: RM 700.00):
     Spindle motor trips breaker or inverter overload mid-heavy cut; electrical reset,
     re-homing machine (~2 hrs downtime @ RM 600.00 + RM 100.00 diagnostic labor).

4. Overstrain Failure (OSF):
   - Planned Fix (Total: RM 100.00):
     20 min labor (RM 10.00) + cutting insert (RM 80.00) + toolholder torque recalibration (RM 10.00).
   - Unexpected Breakdown (Total: RM 1,400.00):
     High torque causes blunt tool to bind, break, and jam workpiece; potential spindle
     misalignment (~3.5 hrs downtime @ RM 1,050.00 + RM 250.00 scrapped part + RM 100.00 damaged clamp/holder).

5. False Alarm (Inspection of healthy machine):
   - Direct Financial Cost: RM 15.00 (30 minutes of technician labor finding no issue).

C. Dataset Weighted Averages (based on ai4i2020 distribution: HDF 32.5%, OSF 27.7%, PWF 26.8%, TWF 13.0%):
- Average Unexpected Breakdown Cost (C_fail): ~RM 936
- Average Planned Fix Cost (C_fix): ~RM 77
- Net Savings per Caught Failure (C_fail - C_fix): ~RM 859

3. TASK 3: EXPLANATIONS & RECOMMENDED ACTIONS (TECHNICIAN PLAYBOOK)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Designed for clear shop-floor action under the "Explain" judging criterion:

1. Tool Wear Failure (TWF):
   - Explanation:
     "Tool nearing end of life: Cumulative cutting time has exceeded 200 minutes,
      indicating a dull or degraded cutting insert."
   - Recommended Action:
     "Inspect the cutting insert for edge wear or micro-chipping; replace with a fresh
      insert before starting the shift."

2. Heat Dissipation Failure (HDF):
   - Explanation:
     "Insufficient cooling: The machine is running slowly and heat is not dissipating
      properly between the workpiece and ambient air (temperature difference < 8.6 K)."
   - Recommended Action:
     "Check coolant fluid levels and nozzle direction; inspect radiator cooling fans
      for metal chip buildup or air blockage."

3. Power Failure (PWF):
   - Explanation:
     "Spindle power out of safe range: Machine power consumption is outside normal
      operating limits (<3.5 kW or >9.0 kW), risking a motor stall or electrical trip."
   - Recommended Action:
     "Check spindle drive belts for slipping or wear, verify electrical supply voltage,
      and ensure spindle bearings rotate freely."

4. Overstrain Failure (OSF):
   - Explanation:
     "High mechanical strain: Heavy cutting torque combined with a worn tool is putting
      excessive mechanical load on the machine spindle."
   - Recommended Action:
     "Replace the cutting insert immediately and verify programmed feed-rate settings
      before resuming heavy cutting cycles."

4. TASK 4: BUSINESS IMPACT & ROI MODEL
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
A. Formula for Judges:
   Net Monthly Savings = [N_fail * Recall * (C_fail - C_fix)] - [False_Alarms * C_false]

   Where:
   - N_fail = Total machine breakdowns occurring per month without predictive AI
   - Recall = Model sensitivity (~80%)
   - Precision = Model precision (~95%)
   - False_Alarms = (N_fail * Recall) * [(1 - Precision) / Precision]
   - Net saving per caught breakdown = C_fail - C_fix = RM 936 - RM 77 = ~RM 859
   - Each breakdown the factory would otherwise suffer is worth ~RM 690 saved
     (80% caught * RM 859 net saving).

B. Monthly Savings Presented as a Range:
   Because the dataset does not specify a real factory's breakdown frequency,
   we present savings across a realistic range of operational conditions:

   | Breakdowns / Month | Breakdowns Caught | False Alarms | Net Monthly Savings |
   | :---: | :---: | :---: | :---: |
   | **5 breakdowns**   | 4.0               | ~0.2         | **about RM 3,400**  |
   | **10 breakdowns**  | 8.0               | ~0.4         | **about RM 6,900**  |
   | **15 breakdowns**  | 12.0              | ~0.6         | **about RM 10,300** |

   * Why 10–15 breakdowns is realistic:
     In a 50-machine plant running 2 shifts/day (~2,000 to 2,500 cutting hours/month),
     experiencing 10 to 15 tool or thermal breakdowns per month represents a 2% to 3%
     failure rate per machine run, aligning closely with typical SME operations.
     This yields **RM 6,900 to RM 10,300 in monthly net savings (~RM 80,000 to RM 120,000/year)**.

C. Worked Example (Using Teammate's Measured Model Numbers):
   - Baseline: 15 breakdowns/month in a 50-machine factory
   - Measured Model Performance: Recall = 80%, Precision = 95%
   1. Failures Caught Early: 15 * 80% = 12 failures
   2. Gross Repair & Downtime Savings: 12 * RM 859 = RM 10,308
   3. False Alarms Generated: 12 * [(1 - 0.95) / 0.95] = 0.63 ≈ ~1 false alarm
   4. Cost of False Alarms: 1 * RM 15 = RM 15
   5. Net Monthly Savings: RM 10,308 - RM 15 = **RM 10,293 / month (~RM 10,300)**

D. The Real Operational Cost of False Alarms (Critical Insight for Pitch):
   While the direct monetary cost of a false alarm is negligible (~RM 15),
   the TRUE bottleneck is **Razif's morning time and cognitive capacity**:
   - Razif has only a 45–60 minute window before full production begins.
   - Each physical inspection takes 20–30 minutes.
   - Therefore, Razif can physically check only **2 to 3 machines** each morning.
   - A single false alarm wastes **33% to 50%** of his entire morning capacity,
     meaning a real impending breakdown elsewhere on the factory floor goes uninspected!
   - This operational reality is why our tool MUST achieve high precision (95%)
     and deliver a strictly prioritized, concise top-3 work-order list rather than
     a cluttered dashboard of noisy alerts.

Two Compelling Story Slides:
1. **The Accuracy Trap Slide**:
   - Our baseline "naive" model achieved 97.3% accuracy, but caught only 20 of 68 failures (29% recall).
   - A model predicting "no failure" always gets 96.6% accuracy while catching zero failures.
   - Demonstrates why Recall and Precision matter, not raw accuracy.
2. **Better Clues Beat Fancier Models Slide**:
   - By engineering 3 physics-informed features (temperature difference, power in Watts, torque * tool wear),
     the exact same model jumped from 20 to 56 caught failures, while false alarms dropped from 6 to 3!

6. HONEST LIMITATIONS & JUDGING DEFENSE (SLIDE READY)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1. **Operating in a Danger Zone, Not Time-Series Forecasting**:
   - Each row is a single machine run with readings and failure status recorded together.
   - There are no sequential timestamps. The model identifies machines operating
     in dangerous physical states ("danger zones") from current readings; it does not
     forecast failures hours or days in advance.
2. **Synthetic Data**:
   - The AI4I 2020 dataset is synthetic. Real sensor data would be noisier and real-world
     scores would be lower. We disclose this honestly to judges.
3. **Assumed Cost Parameters**:
   - All RM figures are explicit modeling assumptions, not audited corporate balance sheets.
4. **CNC Machining Scenario**:
   - The CNC milling machine, spindle, and coolant fan context is our domain mapping based
     on the UCI dataset documentation.
